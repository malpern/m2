M2 Scheduler

Saturday Morning Workflow

How Matt fills next week's schedule in three waves, one Saturday at a time.

Overview

Generate. Reach Out. Confirm.

Every Saturday morning, Matt books next week's training sessions. The system proposes a schedule, sends texts in waves, and helps Matt handle the replies.
📋

Generate

Algorithm proposes sessions based on priority, preferences, and availability.

📱

Reach Out

SMS sent in three timed waves. Standing slots skip outreach entirely.

✅

Confirm

Replies are classified. Matt handles edge cases. Sessions lock in.

Before Saturday

Prep Work (Thursday–Friday)

Two optional steps Matt can do before Saturday morning to make the process smoother.
A
Check Availability
Matt
Go to Schedule → Availability. The grid shows Mon–Fri + Sun, with five one-hour slots a day: 3pm, 4pm, 5pm, 6pm and 7pm. Toggle any slots where you're unavailable next week.
Default availability persists week to week. Use weekly overrides (amber ring) for one-time changes like a dentist appointment on Wednesday.
B
Adjust Priority Rankings
Matt
Go to Schedule → Priority. Three weighted factors determine who gets first pick of time slots:
College Commitment (weight 5) — College-bound athletes score highest
Grade Level (weight 3) — Seniors above freshmen
Effort & Attitude (weight 2) — Behavior score from 1–10

You can also drag to manually reorder. A client you've dragged keeps the position you gave them until you click Reset ranking, which hands them back to the algorithm.
The Main Event

Saturday Morning

The five steps Matt follows every Saturday, typically starting around 9am.
1
Generate the Week
System
Click "Generate Week" on the Schedule page. The system runs a three-pass algorithm:
Pass 1 — Standing Slots: Clients with recurring weekly sessions (e.g., "Mon 3pm, Wed 3pm") are auto-placed first. These are locked — no outreach needed.

Pass 2 — First Session: Every remaining active client gets one session, assigned in priority order. The system tries their preferred day and time first, then cascades to open slots (3pm → 5pm → 6pm).

Pass 3 — College-bound Extras: College-bound athletes with maxSessionsPerWeek ≥ 2 get a second session on a different day.

How time slots are chosen: Currently the system uses the static preferredDays and preferredTime stored on each client's profile. These were auto-detected from historical calendar patterns during import. The system does not yet look at what the client actually booked last week — this is a planned improvement.
2
Review & Adjust
Matt
All generated sessions appear as blue "Proposed" blocks on the calendar. Matt can:
Drag to move — Relocate a session to a different day/time
Click to cancel — Remove a session that doesn't work
Add Session — Manually add a one-off session

This is the last chance to adjust before texts go out. Proposed sessions won't appear on Google Calendar until confirmed — and while outreach is in testing, only test numbers actually receive anything (see Who Can Actually Be Texted Right Now).
3
Send Wave 1
Matt
Go to the Outreach page and click "Send Next Batch". The first 8 highest-priority clients receive a text:
"Hey [firstName], are you free [Day] at [Slot] for a session?"

The system sends via SMS (Twilio). Each message is logged with a timestamp. The outreach dashboard updates to show "Awaiting Reply" for each client.
4
Waves 2 & 3 Send Automatically
System
The remaining clients are texted in two more waves, timed automatically:
Wave 2 — 45 minutes after Wave 1 (next 8 clients)
Wave 3 — 2 hours after Wave 1 (everyone else)
Follow-up — 1 hour after sending, non-responders get a nudge
Expire — 3 hours after sending, unconfirmed slots are released

Not switched on yet. These timings are built and tested, but the automatic trigger behind them is deliberately turned off until outreach goes live — there's no point auto-nudging clients who can't receive texts. Today you send each wave yourself from the Outreach page. Nothing is lost; the waves just wait for you.
5
Handle Replies
Matt
As clients reply, the system classifies each response using AI (Claude Haiku). Most are handled automatically:
"Yes" / "Sounds good" → Auto-confirmed. Session turns green. ✅
"Can't do Friday, how about Monday?" → System checks if Monday is open. If yes, proposes it: "I have Monday at 3pm — does that work?" Client must confirm before it's booked. 🔄
"That doesn't work, what else?" → System finds open slots, ranks by this client's historical pattern, and texts back the top 3 alternatives. 📋
"Not this week" / "Skip me" → Only case that actually cancels. System replies "No problem, we'll get you next week." ❌
"Maybe" / unclear → Flagged for Matt in Mission Control. ❓

Race condition protection: If the system offers a slot and another client books it before this client replies, the system detects the conflict and offers remaining open slots instead of double-booking.
⏱
Typical timeline: Matt clicks Generate Week, reviews the calendar, sends Wave 1 around 9am. By noon, most clients have replied and the week is mostly locked in. Stragglers get follow-ups automatically.

Session Lifecycle

How a Session Moves Through the System

From algorithm proposal to completed training.
Priority Algorithm

Who Gets First Pick

When two clients want the same time slot, the priority score determines who wins.
1
College-Bound Athletes
Worth 50 points by default (10 × weight of 5). The single biggest factor. They also get a second session per week if max allows it.
2
Grade Level
Post-grads score highest at 30 pts, then seniors (4 × 2 × weight 3 = 24 pts). Freshmen get 6 pts. Adults get 0. Designed to prioritize athletes with imminent college deadlines.
3
Effort & Attitude
Matt's 1–10 behavior score, multiplied by weight 2. A client scoring 10/10 gets 20 pts. Rewards athletes who show up and work hard.
4
No-Shows Cost You
Every recorded no-show subtracts 3 points. Two no-shows wipe out most of a perfect effort score, so someone who repeatedly doesn't turn up drifts down the list on their own.
5
Manual Override
Drag-to-reorder bypasses the score entirely, and the order you set sticks. Use it for VIPs or special circumstances. Reset ranking puts everyone back under the algorithm. (There is no separate "pin" — dragging is the pin.)
Standing Slots

Recurring Sessions That Skip Outreach

Some clients train the same day and time every week. These are auto-filled with no text needed.
📌
A standing slot like Mon 3pm, Wed 3pm means the client always trains Monday and Wednesday at 3pm. The scheduler fills these first (Pass 1), before any other clients are considered. No SMS is sent — it's already confirmed.

Set standing slots on each client's detail page. Format: Mon 3pm, Tue 5pm; Thu 3pm, or Monday 15:00.
Wave System

Why Three Waves?

Sending all texts at once would create chaos. Waves let Matt process replies before the next batch goes out.
Wave 0
Standing slots — not a wave at all. These clients are already booked and are never texted to ask.
Wave 1
8 clients — highest priority. Sent when Matt clicks "Send Next Batch." These are the most important sessions to lock in first.
Wave 2
Next 8 clients — auto-sends 45 minutes later. By now, some Wave 1 replies are in and Matt can handle conflicts.
Wave 3
Everyone else — auto-sends 2 hours after Wave 1. The schedule is mostly set; these fill remaining gaps.
Follow-up
After 1 hour — non-responders get a nudge. "Hey, just checking — are you still free for that session?"
Expire
After 3 hours — unconfirmed slots are released and can be reassigned. The client can still confirm later via text.
Command Center

Mission Control

A single screen that shows the state of every client conversation during the Saturday morning booking process.

Available at /outreach/live (or click "Mission Control" from the Outreach page).

1
Pipeline Columns
Every client appears as a card moving left-to-right through five stages: Queued → Texted → Negotiating → Confirmed → Resolved. The "Negotiating" column shows clients in a back-and-forth — alternatives have been proposed and the system is waiting for them to accept. No clicking into detail pages needed — the conversation state is visible on the card itself.
2
Inline Conversations
Each card shows the last 3 messages in the SMS thread — what you sent, what they replied. Cards needing attention glow amber with Confirm/Decline buttons built in.
3
Open Slots Sidebar
A mini calendar on the right shows which time slots are still open (green) vs booked (blue) across the week. Glance at it when handling reschedule requests.
4
Live Summary
Running counts: confirmed, awaiting, needs you, queued. The header shows the real-time state of the entire week at a glance.
Automated Replies

What the System Handles Without Matt

Most decline scenarios are now handled automatically. The system uses AI (Claude Haiku) to classify replies and respond with alternatives.
Confirmed
Client says yes → session auto-confirmed, moves to green. No action needed.
Specific Alt
"How about Monday?" → System checks if that slot is open. If yes, proposes it: "I have Monday at 3pm open — does that work?" Client must confirm before it's booked. If not open, offers other available times.
Wants Options
"That doesn't work" → System finds open slots, ranks by this client's usual pattern, texts back top 3 alternatives.
Picks Offered
Client picks from offered alternatives → System re-verifies slot is still open (race condition check), then confirms.
Skip Week
"Not this week" → Only case that cancels. System replies "No problem, we'll get you next week."
Slot Reserved
When alternatives are offered to a client, those slots are soft-reserved until that client replies or the offer expires (3 hours). Lower-priority clients won't be offered the same slots.
Slot Taken
If a client picks a slot that was booked in the meantime (edge case), the system catches the conflict and offers remaining open slots.
When Matt Needs to Act

What Requires Your Attention

With auto-alternatives, Matt only needs to step in for truly ambiguous cases.
Ambiguous Reply
AI couldn't classify the response. Matt reads the message on the Mission Control card and clicks Confirm or Decline.
Complex Reschedule
Auto-alternatives didn't resolve it (e.g., client wants a time next week, or has a special request). Matt handles manually via Mission Control.
Manual Adjustment
Drag sessions on the calendar to resolve conflicts. If a confirmed session moves, Matt is prompted to text the client.
Session Movement SMS
When moving or cancelling a confirmed session, a pre-drafted text is shown. Matt can edit before sending.
Client Intelligence

What You See on Each Client's Page

Every client detail page shows scheduling intelligence derived from their booking history.
1
Last Week
Shows exactly what this client booked last week — day, time, and what percentage of their all-time sessions use that slot. Times used less than 10% of the time are flagged as "Unusual" in amber, meaning last week was an outlier and not their normal pattern. This helps Matt decide whether to propose the same time again or revert to their typical slot.
2
Schedule Pattern Heatmap
A day × time grid showing every slot the client has used. Core hours (11am–6pm) always show; off-hour times only appear if this client has used them. Color intensity reflects both frequency and recency — sessions in the last 30 days count at full weight, older ones fade with a 60-day half-life.
3
Session History Calendar
A monthly calendar view showing the last 30 days of sessions. Session days are highlighted with colored dots (green = completed, red = cancelled). Gaps of 14+ days between sessions are flagged as breaks with an amber indicator. Click "View all" to expand the full history.
4
Inline Editing
Session rate, type, effort score, standing slot, max sessions per week, and notes are all editable in place — click any value to change it. On the client list, grade and college-bound status are also inline-editable.
5
Ask to Re-Up
When a client is down to 2 sessions or fewer (including zero, or overdrawn), an "Ask to re-up" button appears on their package card. Clicking it opens a draft text — "Hey Micah, you've got 2 sessions left on your package. Want to re-up?" — which you can edit before sending. Nothing goes out until you press Send text. The same button sits on every low package in the amber Package Alerts card under Clients → Reports → Packages, so you can work through them in one pass. Asking doesn't change the balance, and the button is greyed out for a client with no phone number.
6
Pattern Shift Detected
If someone's recorded standing slot no longer matches when they're actually training, an amber "Pattern shift detected" card appears at the top of their page — "now booking Tue 5pm, Fri 3pm instead of Mon 3pm, Thu 3pm". It only speaks up when it's confident: it looks at attended sessions over the last six weeks, needs at least four of those weeks to have sessions, and only counts a day and time that shows up in most of them — so one reschedule won't trigger it. The button is labelled with the new pattern, so pressing "Set to Tue 5pm, Fri 3pm" updates the standing slot in one click. Dismiss hides it for now; it comes back on reload. Nothing changes unless you press the button.
Session Length

Not Every Session Is an Hour

Sessions carry their own length. A longer session takes up more of the afternoon.

Every session has a length. Unless something says otherwise it is one hour, which is what almost every session on the calendar is today.

1
A Client Can Have a Usual Length
On the client's add/edit form there's a Default Session Length dropdown: Use the default (1h), 30m, 45m, 1h, 1h 30m, or 2h. Leave it alone for anyone who trains for an hour.
2
Long Sessions Block the Next Slot
The calendar is built out of one-hour slots. A 90-minute session starting at 3pm occupies both 3pm and 4pm — the system rounds up to whole slots, so nobody else gets offered 4pm. A two-hour session blocks exactly two. A 30-minute session still uses up its whole slot.
3
Where You See It
The week calendar draws each session ending at its real end time, so a long session is visibly taller. The Semi-Group roster cards print it too — "Wednesday, Sep 3 at 5pm · 1h 30m".
⚠️
Being straight with you about the current state: there is no way to change one session's length from the screen yet. Add Session, Generate Week and auto-fill all create one-hour sessions, and the client's default length isn't applied to them yet either. Lengths other than an hour only arrive with imported data. If you need a different length on a specific session, tell Micah — the plumbing is there, the button isn't.
Semi-Groups

Who Actually Showed Up to the Group

Neither Google Calendar nor the Sheets export records who attends a semi-group session. That's what this page is for.

From the Schedule page, click "Semi-Groups" in the row of buttons next to Generate Week. That opens Semi-Group Rosters — one card per group session in the current week.

1
What Shows Up Here
Only sessions whose type is Group, in this week, that haven't been cancelled. There's no week-picker — it's always the current week. If the page is empty it means no group sessions have been generated yet.
2
Adding and Removing People
Each card has a "Add someone…" dropdown listing every client not already on that roster, and an Add button. Anyone you've added has a Remove button next to their name. Changes save immediately — there's no Save button and no confirmation prompt.
3
The Owner Can't Be Removed
The session belongs to one client — that person is always first on the roster and shows a small owner tag instead of a Remove button. If the group really shouldn't be theirs, change or cancel the session on the Schedule page instead.
4
The Roster Doesn't Touch Packages
Completing a group session deducts one session from the owner's package only. Adding three people to a roster does not draw down three packages. The roster is a record of who was there, plus it's what makes the reminder text read "your group session with 2 others" — the text never names the other people.
Reality Check

Who Can Actually Be Texted Right Now

Two separate things stop a text from going out. Both are worth understanding before you wonder why a client went quiet.
1
Outreach is still in testing
System
Until outreach is switched on for real, only a short list of test numbers receives anything — Micah's, essentially. Everyone else's message is prepared, recorded, and then not sent.
Nothing is lost when this happens. The outreach entry drops back to waiting to send rather than pretending it went out, so no follow-up later "expires" a message the client never received and cancels their session out from under them. On the Outreach page the row shows "Send failed:" and the reason in red.
2
Most clients have no phone number on file
Matt
A client is allowed to have no phone number at all — that's a real state, not a broken record. Those clients show an amber "no phone" badge in the client list, and "No phone on file" on their own page.
As of today, 55 of 56 clients are in this state, because the old fake placeholder numbers were cleared out rather than left to look real. A client with no number can't be texted, can't be sent alternatives, and can't be asked to re-up — the Ask to re-up button is greyed out for them. There is no email fallback: adding the number is the fix. Edit the client and type it in.
🔎
Either way, the reason is written down. Settings → System Logs records every attempt, including "Message not sent" and why. See Troubleshooting below.

When Something Looks Wrong

Troubleshooting

The handful of things most likely to go sideways, and what to do about each one. No technical knowledge needed.
?
Google Calendar says Connected, but events aren't showing
Go to Settings → Google Calendar and click Disconnect, then Connect and sign in again. That fixes it almost every time.
The Connected badge is honest now — it doesn't just check that you signed in once, it actually re-checks with Google every time the page loads. So if it says Connected, the connection really is working, and the problem is somewhere else: the session may not be confirmed yet (only confirmed sessions get written to the calendar), or you may be looking at the wrong calendar. M2 reads from your own booking calendar and writes new sessions into the separate Micah – M2 Performance & Therapy calendar, on purpose, so the two don't get tangled together. If a session you confirmed isn't visible, check that that calendar is ticked on in Google Calendar's sidebar before assuming anything is broken.

And if the events are there but look odd — titled with a robot and ending in "— IGNORE JUST TESTING" — that's deliberate. While outreach is in testing, every event M2 creates is marked that way so nobody mistakes it for a real booking. It disappears once outreach goes live.
?
I sent a message but the client didn't get it
This is expected right now for almost everyone. Outreach is still in testing — only a small list of test numbers actually receives texts. Everyone else's message is written down and then held.
To see exactly what happened, go to Settings → System Logs. In the Activity view you'll see "Message not sent". Switch the toggle to Technical and the same line spells out the reason — usually one of:

"no phone number on file" — the client has no number. Edit the client and add one.
"outreach is not live and this … is not on the test allowlist" — nothing is wrong; testing mode is doing its job.
"…is not a usable sms address" — the number on file is too short or mistyped. Fix it on the client's page.

The same reason shows in red on the Outreach page next to that client, as "Send failed: …". Because the message was held rather than faked, nothing later cancels the session on the assumption the client ignored you.
?
A session didn't come off someone's package
A package only goes down when a session is marked completed. If it's still sitting as confirmed, that's the whole answer — mark it completed and the count drops.
Start on the client's page. The package card shows used, total and remaining.

Then check Clients → Reports → Packages. Two things there answer this directly:
• Recent Package Activity — the last 20 changes, each showing the client, what caused it (completed, cancelled, or a manual adjustment), and the balance before and after. If the deduction happened, it's listed here.
• Unreconciled Sessions — sessions that were completed but never came off a package. This is the one to watch: it's where money quietly goes missing. The same count appears on the Reports tab as "sessions need package deduction", in red when it isn't zero.

You can correct a balance by hand from the All Packages table — it's recorded as a manual adjustment, so the history stays honest.
?
Where do I see what the system did?
Settings → System Logs. It's the activity log — the last 200 things the system did, newest first, refreshing itself every 30 seconds.
Leave it on the Activity view; that's plain English. Technical is there for when Micah asks what it says. The filter pills across the top — All, 🛑 Errors, ⚠️ Warnings, ✅ Info — are the quickest way to answer "did anything actually go wrong this morning?": click Errors, and an empty list is good news.
?
How do I report a problem?
Right here on this page. Click anywhere on this guide and a note box opens — type what's wrong and hit Submit.
That does more than leave a sticky note. Each note opens a ticket that Micah sees, and it drops an amber pin at the exact spot you clicked, so the note stays attached to the paragraph it's about. Click any existing pin to read the note and follow its ticket number.

Anything is fair game: something that doesn't match how you actually work, a step that's out of date, a word that means something different in tennis than it does here. Being told the guide is wrong is the point of the guide having a comment box.
